# S4 O-015: the Forge and Quicksilver trails under deutan vision, read on drawn frames

## 1. What was asked and how it was decided

O-015 asked whether the Forge and Quicksilver trail tokens should change to the source game's lighter pair, A: `Pal.Keeper.Forge` #FFC17A and `Pal.Keeper.Quicksilver` #B3FB60. The reason was that, from the palette values under simulated deutan vision, the Forge trail sits 8.5 from the enemy muzzle flash, where `settings.md` R-SET-11 asks 15, and 16.5 from the telegraph, where it asks 20. The other option, B, was to keep the confirmed palette (D-008) and rely on the Danger outlines setting (R-SET-10).

D-059 decided it: the two trails keep their colours, and from now on a colour-vision debt between a player mark and an enemy mark is judged on a drawn frame by the pipeline of this note, reported beside the palette figure. This note is the evidence D-059 asks for.

## 2. Method

The method is R-SET-11's and its test's, `Dusktracer.Settings.ColourVision` (T-SET-11, `Source/Dusktracer/Tests/Settings/DTSettingsTests.cpp`):
1. An 8-bit sRGB colour goes to linear RGB on the sRGB curve.
2. The Machado, Oliveira, Fernandes 2009 matrix is applied at full severity, and each channel is clamped to [0, 1].
3. The result goes to XYZ (D65, with the test's coefficients) and then to CIE L*a*b*.
4. The distance between two colours is CIE76, the plain Euclidean distance.

The limits are `C.SETTINGS.CVD_MUZZLE_DELTA_E_MIN` against the muzzle flash and `C.SETTINGS.CVD_DELTA_E_MIN` against every other enemy mark.

Applied per pixel, the same pipeline gives a simulated frame. Applied to the median colour of a set of pixels picked on a drawn frame, it gives a drawn-frame figure.

The tool is `Tools/cvd_frame.py`. It uses the standard library only and runs as `python -I`. It reads a frame with `Tools/image_check.py`'s PNG reader and the tokens through `Tools/dtpalette.py`. It has three commands:
- `palette`: the palette table.
- `sim`: a simulated frame.
- `measure`: the pixel sets, their medians and the distances.

The windows below are its named defaults, and each one can be overridden on the command line (`--window`, `--exclude`, `--guard`). `Tools/tools_test.py` has a class for it, `CvdFrame`:
- its matrices are read out of the test file, so the two cannot drift;
- the 8.5 figure comes out of the palette;
- a small synthetic frame has known sets, medians and distances;
- grey is unchanged by the simulation;
- it runs under `-I`.

Its palette figure for Forge against the muzzle under deutan is 8.464, which is the test's 8.5 within 0.1.

## 3. The frames

Three scenes in `Tools/Scenes`, each run by hand and never by the gate:
- `o015_forge`: map `?DTPatron=Forge`, blessing `Blessing.Forge.Slam.Key`. The Hammer Key would turn rounds into melee; the Slam Key fires only on a dash landing, every Nth melee hit or a hit that costs HP, and none of those happens here.
- `o015_quicksilver`: map `?DTPatron=Quicksilver?DTUnlock=Keeper.Quicksilver`, blessing `Blessing.Quicksilver.Vector.Key`, which acts only on a dash.
- `o015_radiance`: map `?DTPatron=Radiance?DTUnlock=Keeper.Radiance`, blessing `Blessing.Radiance.Lance.Key`. Every fifth round is charged and pierces; it is still a round.

All three copy `tracer_crossing`: four weapons at level 8, 48 Turrets, 12 Armored Rushers, enemy ring fire, God mode, and the hero walked to (450, 0). The tail takes the primary Path's colour once a blessing of it is owned (`tracer_art.md` R-TRC-03), so each scene names its Patron on the map. Every log read `DT.Keeper.Stats p0 primary=<Path> ... owned=[ <blessing> ]`.

Each shot used seed 0, the director off, the sim held at 5.6 s (step 336 at 60 Hz), 1920x1080 (36 px/m) and `en`, through `Tools/Frames.ps1`.

The step was chosen in six trial launches of `o015_forge`:
- 5.0 s and 5.4 s: aim lines, no muzzle flash.
- 5.7 s: the flashes were mostly gone.
- 6.3 s: aim lines, no flash.
- 6.45 s: three bright flashes and no telegraph.
- 5.6 s: three Turrets fire with fresh flashes, Rusher bands are live at the hero, and played tails cross them and the enemy rings.

The Forge and Quicksilver frames share their geometry. The Radiance frame does not: the Lance Key changes what is hit, its top Turrets still aim at 5.6 s, and it serves only for the Forge tail against the Radiance tail.

The three variants of each scene:
- **today**: the `dev/s4` tokens, outlines off.
- **B**: today's tokens, with `DT.UI.Set outline 1` appended to the commands. That command sets the live setting only and saves nothing (`Source/Dusktracer/Settings/DTMenuSubsystem.cpp`; a tool run with `-unattended` has no settings file).
- **A**: the two token rows of `04_Presentation.md` §3 set to #FFC17A and #B3FB60, `DTPaletteGen.h` regenerated with `python -I Tools/gen_constants.py`, the editor rebuilt, then shot.

The A build lived on a throwaway branch, `tmp/o015-a`, which was deleted after this note was committed. As history:
- `4c25b18` held the three scenes; today and B were shot there. Its game code and content were `dev/s4` 0f8a973's.
- `b880437` held the token change; A was shot there.

To rebuild A:
1. On a scratch branch, change those two cells of the table.
2. Run `python -I Tools/gen_constants.py`, then `Tools\Build.bat`.
3. Run `& Tools\Frames.ps1 -Scene o015_forge -Tag A`, and the same for the other two scenes.
4. Throw the branch away and rebuild.

The frames, the simulated frames, the sheets (whole frames and 640x360 crops enlarged three times, today / B / A by plain / deutan, and a Forge against Radiance sheet) and the mask pictures are not in the repo. They were made in `Saved\S4Report\O-015\` of the Visual session's worktree, with a README that lists them.

## 4. How the pixel sets were picked

Every set is a window on today's plain frame, as hue (degrees), minimum saturation and minimum value. The same sets are reused, pixel for pixel, on the B and A frames of the scene.

A difference mask between today and A was tried and dropped, for two reasons:
- Two launches of the same scene at the same step differ in about 13,400 pixels by more than 6 levels and in 1,410 by more than 24 (render noise, mostly shadow and anti-aliasing).
- The Quicksilver change is smaller than that noise.

The windows:
- **tail**: Forge 26 to 50, Quicksilver 70 to 115, Radiance 38 to 62; saturation at least 0.25, value at least 0.45.
- **muzzle**: 5 to 24; at least 0.45 and 0.55.
- **telegraph**: 352 to 8; at least 0.6 and 0.45. In the Forge and Quicksilver frames at this step these pixels are the Rusher bands; the Radiance frame also holds aim lines.
- **round**: 315 to 345; at least 0.45 and 0.55. These are the rims; the Turret optics are near white at their centre and fall mostly outside.

Each enemy set leaves out pixels within 3 px of a tail pixel. Every set leaves out the HUD (y < 120, and x < 340 for y > 850). The median is taken per channel, and the figure is the distance between the medians.

## 6. What the sheets show

**Forge.** Plain, the tails are short amber streaks behind white heads, the bands are red and the enemy rings are magenta. Under deutan the bands turn olive-yellow, the same family as the amber tails. Where a tail lies on a band, the two read as one warm smear, but each band keeps its black edge and each ring its dark middle. Under A the tails are paler and yellower: hard to see on the whole frame, visible in the crop.

**Quicksilver.** The lime tails turn the same olive-yellow under deutan. A looks the same as today.

**Outlines (B).** In B every enemy round gets a light grey disc with a black edge, and each band a light edge. No outline is drawn for a muzzle flash. Where a Turret's fresh rounds sit on its muzzle, their discs cover the flash, which is why the muzzle median in B reads #DDDCDD.

**Forge against Radiance.** Plain, today, the Forge tails are orange and the Radiance tails pale yellow, easy to tell apart. Under A the Forge tails turn pale yellow too, and under deutan the two rows could not be told apart at that size.

## 7. What it does not show

- No person with deutan vision looked at any frame. The simulation is a model of one form of colour vision, at full severity.
- Every frame is one held step. Nothing was seen in motion, where a tail is a moving streak and a flash lasts `C.TRC.MUZZLE_S`.
- One moment of one fight: one seed, one weather (Dusk), one size (36 px/m).
- The muzzle set is small: 120, 148 and 31 pixels of about two million. The muzzle figures rest on that.
- B's muzzle figure measures the outline disc over the flash, not the flash.
- The drawn-frame figures depend on the windows chosen. No other windows were tried; the pixel counts and, in the Visual session's folder, the mask pictures show what each set holds.
- The Radiance frame's geometry differs from the Forge frame's, so their tail comparison is between two frames, not one.
- Two launches of the same scene differ by render noise; each scene's sets were picked once, on today's frame, and reused.
