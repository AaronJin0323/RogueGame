# D-066 — The game's sound is stone and bronze; the voices are the Six; the old machine is the Keep

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decider:** scheduler, on the owner's word. Sent three directions to choose from by ear on 2026-11-11, the owner answered: "你来选择最适合游戏背景的" (you choose the one that best fits the game's setting)
- **Class:** RULE (presentation; the character of sound). It closes O-016 and applies D-056's method
- **Status:** applied: the Sound session makes the interface family in it; no sound of it is merged yet

## Context
The first ten interface sounds (soft generated tones, every numeric check passing) were rejected by the owner for style: the sounds should fit the game's story and setting and feel epic and grand while staying as simple as possible, not harsh, not annoying (D-056, Result). The Sound session then wrote what the world sounds like from the fiction (`Validation/S4_audio_world.md` on `dev/s4`: a temple that is a machine; stone, bronze, a great hall, the voices of the Six, the old systems, ceramic) and made three directions of the same four sounds: A, stone and bronze; B, the voices; C, the old machine. No agent can hear. The owner left the choice to the scheduler.

## Options
- A — Stone and bronze for everything: things struck in a great stone hall.
- B — The voices for everything: a low choir on open fifths with a horn under it.
- C — The old machine for everything: relays, latches, a mechanism waking.
- D — All three, each with a meaning: the materials are one world, and what a sound is made of says who is acting.

## Decision
D, with A as the base.

1. **Stone and bronze is the base, and the whole interface family is made in it.** Cards are carved tablets and the Keepers' gifts. Stone is for what is small, frequent or refused (the cursor, a value stepped, a screen drawn aside, a refusal); bronze is for what is granted (a hand of cards opening, a card taken, a seal pressed). The hall rings only on the rare ones.
2. **The voices are kept for the Six themselves:** a Keeper chosen, a blessing's signature, an Intercession. They are not spent on the interface.
3. **The old machine is the Keep's own acts and the Reclaimer's tools:** weapons, the dash, mechanisms, and a warning's material where iron fits better than bronze.
4. Kills are ceramic breaking, as the sound-world page has it. A warning belongs to the same world by its material and stands apart by its size, in the band D-056 keeps for danger.
5. The `heard` column of the sound catalog has four values: `no`, `rejected`, `yes`, and `delegated` (the owner left the choice to the scheduler, who accepted it; the files were sent to the owner). The import accepts `yes` and `delegated`. Every family is still written out as audition files and passed to the owner; it is offered, and it does not wait.

Why: a choir on every cursor move would spend the one sound that should mean a Keeper is acting, and it is the source most at risk of reading as an organ; the machine alone would make the Keep a factory and lose the temple; stone and bronze alone would leave the Six and the Keep's systems without a voice. By the Sound session's own figures A's four sounds stand furthest apart (14.0 dB at the closest pair, against 13.5 and 8.6), and its most frequent sound is the driest, which is what "simple, never tiring" asks.

## Hypothesis
We believe sounds made of the world's own materials read as this game and not as a system's beeps, and that giving each material one meaning lets a player tell who is acting by ear; measured by the owner's hearing of each family as it is made (accepted, or what was wrong), and at the first kit that carries sound by whether a tester can say what a sound was for; reverted, for a family or for the whole grammar, on the owner's word after hearing it. The numbers of D-056 still gate level, band and distance; they do not judge this.
