# D-042 — One change to the save at a time: reread, change and write under a lock held for that change

- **Date:** 2026-11-10
- **Decider:** scheduler (delegated, D-009)
- **Class:** BUG (the fix), with a RULE line in the spec: `meta.md` R-MET-11 now states the reread and the lock
- **Status:** applied on `fix/d042-save-lock`, from `dev/s3` at `9fafa8f`

## Context
Review entry #0 of `Validation/S3_review_fixes.md`: two game processes on one save, and the last writer wiped the other's banks and purchases. Its fix (`8bb490d`) rereads the file before a bank or a purchase when another process wrote it. `Validation/S3_verify.md` then found what that leaves: a bank and a purchase each read the file, change the profile and write it, with nothing between the read and the write. Two processes that both read before either writes still lose one change, with no log line. Seen there once in ten trials of two processes banking by their own timing, 10 ms apart.

The spec was behind the code as well: R-MET-11 said how a write is atomic and nothing of the reread.

Measured before this change, on real game processes held open on one save and told to bank at one instant (`Tools/TwoWindowsCheck.ps1 -Race`, the call a run's end makes): with two processes 40 of 40 trials lost a bank (40 of 80 banks), with four 40 of 40 (120 of 160). Forced, the window is hit every time; the one-in-ten above is what unforced timing gives.

## Options
From `S3_verify.md`:
- A — Leave it: two windows on one save is an edge. A lost run or purchase stays possible and silent.
- B — A lock for the length of one change: a sibling lock file opened with no sharing; reread, change, write, close. Needs a wait limit and a rule for a lock that cannot be had.
- C — One game per save: a second launch refuses to start or starts with no save. Closes this and the stale balance, at the cost of a new message and a rule for a crashed first window.

## Decision
B, made by the scheduler. The details:

1. **The lock.** `<save>.lock`, beside the save, opened with no sharing (`CreateFileW`, share mode zero, delete on close; `open` and `flock` elsewhere) and held only from before the reread to after the write of one change. The operating system closes the handle when the process ends, however it ends, so a crash leaves no lock and nothing for a later launch to clean up; a file of that name that nobody holds blocks nothing. `FDTMetaLock`.
2. **One change.** `FDTMetaSession::Change`: take the lock, reread the file if another launch wrote it, make the change on that profile, write atomically (R-MET-11 as it was), let the lock go. A bank and a purchase are each one such change. A purchase's checks and its price are taken on the file's profile, inside the lock.
3. **The wait.** Up to `C.SAVE.LOCK_WAIT_MS` (250 ms), asking every `C.SAVE.LOCK_RETRY_MS` (5 ms). Four launches banking at one instant finished within a median of 16 ms of each other, so a change holds the lock for about 5 ms and the wait is some fifty times that; it is a quarter of a second, once, on a run-over screen or a button press, and only when another launch is writing.
4. **A lock that cannot be had.** No change is lost in silence.
   - A bank is made in memory, so the run-over screen reads the right award and balance, and is **kept as a change**, not as a profile: at this session's next save point (its next bank, its next purchase, or exit) the lock is asked for again, the file is reread, and the kept run is made again on what the file then holds. The log carries a warning and the run's telemetry a `save_deferred` record.
   - A purchase is refused: `EDTTreeBuy::Busy`, nothing spent, a log warning. The player can press again. A purchase is not kept, because a kept purchase could meet a balance another launch has spent by the time it is written.
   - A bank still waiting at exit, when the lock is still not free, is lost. That is the one path left where a change goes, and it is said: an error line in the log. It needs a process that holds the lock from a run's end to the game's exit, which a healthy launch holds for milliseconds.
5. **A failed write joins the same rule.** A change that reached the lock but whose write failed (R-MET-13) used to stay in memory as a whole profile, and the next write put that profile over whatever another launch had written since. It is kept as a change now, and made again on the file's profile like a deferred bank. A kept purchase is checked again there (R-MET-26) and dropped with a warning if it no longer passes: its price was never taken from the file.
6. **Two lines, added in a second pass on the same branch.** `ST_Tree` `Tree.State.Busy` is the detail of the Contracts row whose press was refused, until the next press or until the page is left (`vigil.md` R-VIG-19). `ST_UI` `Summary.Waiting` is drawn under the run-over panel for a bank kept in memory, in the Discovery toast's slot and in its place for that run (`meta.md` R-MET-06). Both are invented in en and zh-Hans, marked `[invented]` in Context and listed for a native reader in `Validation/S3_vigil.md`.
7. **A bank lost at exit has no line.** A next launch cannot know of it: the launch that lost it has ended, the save could not be written, and nothing else is kept. Telling the next launch would take a file of its own beside the save, written at exit and read and removed at the next start, and a place on the hub for its line. Not built; the log's error line is the record. (Built afterwards on `fix/s3-minors-4`: the exit leaves `<save>.lost`, the next launch reads and removes it and its hub says `Hub.Notice.RunLost` once; `meta.md` R-MET-11, T-MET-51, `vigil.md` R-VIG-21.)

Known and left, as the scheduler set the scope:
- **The stale balance.** A window that sat idle while another launch wrote draws the balance, the Tree and the Shrine it last read, until its own next bank or purchase rereads the file. A purchase there is checked against the file, so a balance that reads as enough can be refused as short. Read from the code, not looked at.
- **Two games on one save** stay allowed. Option C is not taken.

## Hypothesis
We believe holding an exclusive lock from the reread to the write makes two changes to one save land one after the other, whatever their timing, because the second cannot read until the first has written; and that keeping a change that could not be written as a change makes it land on top of the file and not over it. Measured by `Dusktracer.Meta.SaveWindow` (T-MET-49: a bank and a purchase forced inside another session's window), `Dusktracer.Meta.SaveLock` (T-MET-50) and 40 trials of real launches banking at one instant, which must lose none. Reverted to a longer wait or to option C if a human session reports a hitch at a run's end or a purchase that did nothing with one game open.

## Result
- T-MET-49 on the code before the fix: fails. The bank made inside the window is gone (two runs of three in the file), and the purchase said bought and is not in the file. After: passes; the bank inside the window is kept and lands at the next bank, the purchase is refused as busy and buys when asked again.
- T-MET-50: passes. A held lock is waited for `C.SAVE.LOCK_WAIT_MS` and no longer; a stale lock file blocks nothing; with the lock held a purchase is refused, a bank is kept, and once free the kept run lands on what the holder wrote; a change a failed write left lands on another window's changes, and a purchase that cannot be made again is dropped, not charged.
- Real launches, `Tools/TwoWindowsCheck.ps1 -Race 40`, before and after on the same script:

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Two launches, 40 trials | 40 trials lost a bank, 40 of 80 banks | 0 trials, 0 of 80 |
| Four launches, 40 trials | 40 trials lost a bank, 120 of 160 banks | 0 trials, 0 of 160; none had to be kept in memory |
| Lock held by another for longer than the wait, a bank | no lock existed | said in the log, the file untouched; the launch's next bank writes both runs |
| The same, a purchase | no lock existed | result `Busy`, balance and Tree as planted |
| The same to the launch's exit, a real bot run | no lock existed | the log says kept, the JSONL holds `save_deferred`, the log says lost at exit, no file is written |

- The four forced orders of T-MET-48 still pass. The gate line and seed 9107's hashes are in the report of the branch's tip.

## Impact (dependency sweep)
- Rules: `meta.md` R-MET-11 (the reread, the lock, the wait, what a busy lock does), R-MET-06 (the waiting line), R-MET-13, R-MET-16 and R-MET-26 reworded to it; T-MET-48 loses its "not covered" line; T-MET-49 and T-MET-50 added, T-MET-06 extended. `vigil.md` R-VIG-19 and T-VIG-19 hold the busy line. `telemetry.md` lists `save_deferred`.
- Text: two rows, `ST_Tree` `Tree.State.Busy` and `ST_UI` `Summary.Waiting`, en and zh-Hans, through `Tools/loc_build.py`.
- Constants: `C.SAVE.LOCK_WAIT_MS`, `C.SAVE.LOCK_RETRY_MS`, both new and technical (`number_change_log.csv`). `C.GATE.TEST_FLOOR` 524 to 526 for two tests.
- Code: `Meta/DTMetaSession` (`FDTMetaLock`, `Change`, `Flush`, the kept changes), `Meta/DTTree` (`Buy` as one change, `EDTTreeBuy::Busy`), `Meta/DTMetaProfile.h` (`FDTRunAward::bDeferred`, `Waiting`), `Meta/DTRunRecorder` and `Telemetry/DTTelemetryCore` (`save_deferred`), `Meta/DTMetaSubsystem` (the exit's flush and its error line; `DT.Meta.BankWhen`, debug only), `Meta/DTTreeTab` (the busy line), `Settings/DTMenuScreen` and `Settings/DTMenuSubsystem` (the waiting line).
- The save format does not change: no field, no version. A launch alone on its save writes the same bytes as before; it opens and closes one more file per change.
- Telemetry: `save_deferred{change waiting}` is written only when a lock was busy past its wait, so a tool run alone on its save writes none and R-MET-16 holds.
- Files beside the save: `DTMeta.sav.lock` exists only while a change is being made on Windows. `Tools/PackageFiles/Export_Logs.ps1` and the checks that list the save folder were not changed; none names the file.
- An older build on the same save takes no lock: two builds across this change race as before it.

## Not verified
- A process killed while it holds the lock. The release is the operating system's (a closed handle) and was not staged: the hold is a few milliseconds. What is tested is the nearest thing a test can make, a lock file nobody holds.
- The non-Windows branch (`open`, `flock`) is written and was never compiled or run: macOS is deferred (D-007).
- The lock on a save folder that is a network share, or one synced by a cloud client: sharing modes are the file system's there.
- An antivirus or indexer that opens `DTMeta.sav.lock` itself would read as a busy lock for as long as it holds the file. Not seen in 240 banks here; not staged.
- The busy line was read on the page's own object (`FDTTreeTab`, T-VIG-19) and the refusal on a launch through `DT.Tree.BuyWhen`, which makes the same call a node's Accept makes; no key was pressed on the Contracts page of a launch with the lock held, and no frame with the line was drawn or measured. It is shorter than `Tree.State.ReadOnly`, which the same room holds, in both languages.
- The waiting line on the run-over screen: the text resolves in both languages (T-MET-06) and the screen picks it from the award's flag; no frame was drawn or looked at, and no test opens the run-over screen on a kept bank.
- Both zh rows are invented and no native reader has read them.
- The stale balance of an idle second window, as before: read from the code.
- More than four launches at once, and a purchase racing a purchase by timing: the timed race is banks only. Purchases are covered by the forced interleaving (T-MET-49) and the four forced orders (T-MET-48).

## Swap-out point
Option C (one game per save) replaces the wait and the busy rules if two windows on one save turn out to be common or the stale balance is reported: the lock then is taken once at launch and held for the session's life, and `Change` keeps its reread and write.
