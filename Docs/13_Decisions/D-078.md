# D-078 — A Scatter Shell ring bursts once: a fault of the session build, mended on `main` before session A

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decider:** scheduler (delegated, D-009), on the Mechanics module session's Draft 16 (`Validation/scatter_shell_ring_plan.md`, `plan/scatter-shell-ring` 7ffa23b) and its trace of the run files
- **Class:** BUG (the code gives `Mod.ScatterShell` two generations of rings where its own catalog line says one, `09_Catalog/modifiers.csv`; `modifiers.md` R-MOD-05 gains the clause that says so). It is taken on the stricter path all the same: a hypothesis and a reading
- **Status:** applied on `main` (2026-11-13); the scene met as written, the six-run clause missed as written and met as amended after its reading (rulings 9 to 12); the session A package is stale until it is built again

## Context
- A weapon may emit `C.WEAPON.SPAWN_RATE_MAX_PER_S` (400) things in any one second; rounds, children and chain jumps share the count, and what is over it is dropped and counted in the run file only (`weapons.md` R-WPN-09).
- `Mod.ScatterShell` bursts a round into a ring of 6 children when its flight ends. Its entry carries no limit of its own, so it stops at `C.MOD.SPLIT_GENERATIONS_MAX` (2): a child bursts into a ring again. One round asks for 1 + 6 + 36 = 43 emissions. On the Ring Emitter, whose rounds fly in every direction and mostly end their flight unspent, that is 430 a second at level 1 and 1 344 at level 8, against 400.
- Read from run files that exist (bots, a bound). On the session build 842259e the card "Scatter Shell on the Ring Emitter" is offered in 144 of 285 runs, about two and a half times in such a run; the steered bot takes it in six, and all six are refused 0.08 to 0.43 of what the weapon asks from the socket to the run's end (median 0.29). No other weapon and socket set passes 0.05 at skill 1.0. On `dev/s2` three runs with the socket carry 512 423 of 517 059 refusals of a fifteen-run battery.
- By code and arithmetic, not seen by anyone: with the socket the weapon sits at the cap from its first level, so a level that adds rounds or speed adds nothing, and at levels 1 to 5 it puts out more than the design gives it (about 85 rounds' worth a second against 34 to 64). Nothing on the screen says a weapon is being refused.
- The source's scatter children do not burst again, and its card says "a bullet bursts into 6". The port's catalog line says "(one generation)".
- Both the weapon and the modifier are S1 starters: the session A package holds the fault from a tester's first run.

## Options
- **One generation** (the draft's lever 2a): a round born of a Scatter Shell ring never bursts into a ring again. One round asks for 7; the Ring Emitter at level 8 asks for 219 a second.
- **The cap's number**: it would have to pass 1 344, about two thirds of the bullet pool from one weapon. The cap is doing what it is for.
- **The source's short child as well** (2c): one more change to the build people are about to play, and not needed for the budget.
- **A mark on the HUD when a weapon is refused**: honest, and it would tell the player the card is broken.
- **Hold it until after session A**, with a line in the kit: the session would then read the fault and not the weapon in every run that takes the card, and the card is offered in half of all runs.

## Decision
One generation, on `main`, before session A.

1. `modifiers.md` R-MOD-05 gains: a round born of a Scatter Shell ring never bursts into a ring again; `Mod.ScatterShell` makes one generation of rings, as its catalog line says. T-MOD-05 follows.
2. Everything else stays: Split Core's children, a Split Core child that bursts one ring when its flight ends, a Keeper's own split, the cap at 400 as the guard, the children's flight.
3. It is a defect a tester would hit, so it lands before session A (D-050's line is what a tester meets, not the calendar): the card is put in front of the player in every second run, every run that takes it is over the bar, and what it changes is what the session asks about, a weapon's growth and a build's strength.
4. The job: `s1/scatter-one-generation` from `main`, the whole gate; one new test scene (a level 8 Ring Emitter with Scatter Shell on an empty field spawns every emission it asks for), so `C.GATE.TEST_FLOOR` rises by one; `Tools/gate_arms.csv` is edited in the branch for `Random` and `Steered:Tide:Frost`, which socket Scatter Shell on seed 9107, with this record named, and the other three values must not move.
5. One tool with it, printed only: the battery summary prints the worst refused share by weapon and socket set, and the reader ends with a line that says whether any passes 0.05, so that the next such pairing is found by a reader.
6. After it lands the scheduler rebuilds the session A package alone, replaces the zip and the tag, and tells the owner. The owner's own checkout is built again.
7. The later branches take it with the next carry. D-074's judged pair holds the socket on three seeds, so its two windows are read again after the carry, printed, and reported before anything else lands there if one falls under its bar. Each branch's own seed 9107 values are read then.
8. Not decided here: whether a refused weapon should show on the screen (R-WPN-09's "never silently" is kept only in the run file); the children's long flight on a long-lived weapon. Each waits for people or for a reading.

## Hypothesis
We believe that a Scatter Shell ring that bursts once keeps the Ring Emitter inside its emission budget, because one round then asks for 7 emissions and not 43; measured on `main` by the six runs of the D-045 battery in which the bot sockets Scatter Shell on the Ring Emitter, run again on the mended build; judged: the weapon's refused share after the socket is under 0.05 in every one of the six (today 0.43, 0.42, 0.34, 0.25, 0.17, 0.08), and the test scene spawns every emission it asks for; printed and judged nowhere: the weapon's damage a minute after the socket beside today's, each run's end and outcome; reverted if any of the six still passes 0.05, with a report of what is still refused. No survival clause is judged or tuned (D-050).
