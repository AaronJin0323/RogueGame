## Context
- The first visual wave drew shapes: the plate, four enemy hulls, the hero's figure, tracers, the HUD, a hit and a kill. It worked from a list of 212 gaps between an art book and the game, at what its plan calls silhouette grade, and every package was accepted by machine checks on held frames: a contrast ratio, a count of pixels, a golden. The plan for the second wave proposed "state before surface", and D-057 held it until the owner had sat or session A was read.
- The art book all of that cited was the wrong one. It was an early set of boards (version 0.3, two canvases the owner holds). The owner's art direction is a later and far larger document: "RogueHero 美术指导", version 2.4 of 2026-10-22, 180 pages, a file the owner holds outside the repository. It says of itself that the old pages are history only.
- No acceptance of the first wave put the game's frame beside a frame of either book. The scheduler did it for the first time on 2026-11-12, after the owner's words, and that is the scheduler's own miss: it had looked at many frames for what could be read in them and never for what they looked like.
- What the art direction shows for a fight (its pages on the fight's space, the game camera, light and architecture): quiet grey stone slabs with joints, wear and a little standing water; stone blocks and drains at the edge with warm lamps; enemies that are old machines, pale ceramic and bone-white plates over dark mechanisms, each with a magenta optic; a hero who is a person, hooded and masked, dark undersuit and pale plates, firing from a rack on the back under the Keeper's lamp; white-cored rounds with cold tails against dark-cored magenta enemy rounds; red warnings lying on the ground. Its camera shows about 27 m across with the hero about 60 pixels tall.
- What the game shows: a flat mid-brown floor with faint lines, no mood of light and no glow, dark box-like enemies, a pale figure about forty pixels tall, and a camera that shows about 71 m across.
- The art direction also carries numbers that the game can be held to: lit stone no brighter than a luminance of 0.20, a round's core at 0.85 or more, rounds against ground at 3 to 1 or more, a fixed exposure, bloom for emissive parts only, no motion blur, chromatic aberration or depth of field.

## Options
- **The look first, designed by the Visual session under the art direction 2.4** (this record).
- **The plan as it stood**: statuses, pickups and weapons at silhouette grade first, surface later. It answers what the sim knows and the screen does not say; it does not answer what the owner said.
- **Wait for session A.** Session A reads the S1 build, which has none of this art, and cannot say anything about it.

## Decision
The look first, in ten items.

1. The guidance is the owner's art direction 2.4. The early boards are history and are cited no more. Every plate citation of the first wave and the list of 212 gaps were read against the early boards; the Visual session reads the art direction whole and says what of that list still stands.
2. It is guidance and not a model, in the owner's words. Its pictures are not copied. The Visual session designs this game's look itself: it takes the direction (the world, the materials, what each thing is for, the contract of what must be read) and makes its own shapes, surfaces, light and motion, adjusts them by its own eye, and aims past the pictures, not at them. A design that only repeats a picture of the art direction is not accepted as done.
3. D-057's hold is lifted: the owner has sat.
4. The order starts from what the owner named: floor, light and atmosphere first, with the exposure and the glow that everything else is then judged in; the hero's and the enemies' models second; rounds and effects third. Drawing the sim's states follows, or rides with a package where it costs little. The Visual session's plan may argue another order with reasons.
5. How a look is accepted. Every look package begins with the Visual session's own design for it, as a sheet made first: what the thing is in this world, what the design takes from the direction, where it departs and why, and what would make it better than the picture. It ends with the game's frame at the game's real size. The scheduler looks at the frame for what it is: whether it holds as a picture, whether it belongs to this world, whether it reads. The art direction's picture of the same subject is shown beside it as the bar to clear, to ask whether ours is the better one, never whether the two are alike. The frame goes to the owner. The machine checks stay as guards against regressions; none of them is the acceptance of a look.
6. The readability contract stands and now has the art direction's own numbers beside the game's: danger told by shape, the contrast gates, lit stone under 0.20, a round's core over 0.85. Where one of the art direction's pictures breaks its own contract, the contract wins.
7. The camera is the scheduler's to decide, on the owner's word. The art direction's game camera (about 27 m across, the hero about 60 pixels) is the starting point. A study first: one staged fight at three framings (today's, the art direction's, one between), each beside the art direction's fight, with what each does to what a player can see (the spawn ring, how far ahead danger is read, the plate's edge). The Mechanics session names the readings each framing makes stale. The ruling is a record of its own.
8. The enemies' hulls. The art direction draws them pale over dark; the game holds dark hulls by D-010 and a token. The look's plan brings that as a call with frames in both, under the contract of item 6; nothing moves before it.
9. What does not move: no AI-sourced or downloaded asset; everything in the game is generated by scripts in this repository, since the repository is public. The art direction, its text and its images never enter the repository. The wave's budgets of D-057 ruling 4 stand (8.0 ms of proxied GPU and 6.5 ms of game thread at p95); the look will spend GPU, so a reading on a quiet machine is owed before the first look package lands.
10. Work judged by eye (meshes, materials, light) is done by the strongest model at its highest effort, one agent at a time, in small commits, as the owner ruled for visual modelling on 2026-11-11. Session A is not touched or delayed: it reads `main`. D-076's mend goes on beside this.

## Hypothesis
We believe that building floor, light and atmosphere, then models, then rounds and effects, each designed by the Visual session under the owner's art direction and held to a bar above its pictures, moves the owner's verdict away from "crude", because those three are what the owner named and what the pair of frames shows; measured by the owner's look at each package's frame and at the next sitting; re-planned if the owner says the first package did not move it.
