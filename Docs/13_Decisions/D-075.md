# D-075 — A measuring arm for the bot: it holds its crowd at the carried weapon's reach

- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decider:** scheduler (delegated, D-009), on a draft by the Mechanics module session (D-055 item 4: draft 15, `Validation/blade_halo_next_plan.md`), accepted on 2026-11-12 with the rulings at the end
- **Class:** SYSTEM, the measuring tools (a bot arm, off by default, on `dev/s2`). It changes no rule of play and no number. It is a method decision: what a reading taken with the arm may and may not decide is ruling 4
- **Status:** built twice, missed its own clause both times and taken out (Result); nothing of it is landed

## Context
- D-071 put Blade Halo on the source's rhythm and missed: the build read 0.89 of its hold before the boss against a bar of 0.9 and the port's 0.80 on the same base, and the weapon's hits a minute did not move (61 against 60, the hold's drones 247).
- The run files say why: after the pick the nearest enemy stands at a median of 201 uu, and the blades reach about 200 (the orbit 150, the contact 30, the enemy's own radius). The bot holds its crowd one step outside the weapon and never closes it. It is the same on every pair.
- On the pair that does not live on a round's children the source's halo stands at its bars, printed only: 0.93 before the boss and 0.90 in the fight. So the larger fault of the instrument here is the bot's standoff, not the pair.
- D-074 item 7 says an evolution that deletes its weapon's emission is not built again without a reading, by a bot that uses the weapon or by people, that shows it holds a run. No such bot exists.

## Options
- **The arm first** (this record): a bot that steps in, as a measuring arm beside the carry arm, then the halo read with and without it, and the form ruled on those columns.
- **The additive form now**: the drones keep their bolts and gain the blades. By arithmetic a little over 1.0, and nothing changes in how the build already kills. It would land a weapon that is the plain one with a ring this bot does not use.
- **The source's form held for people**: nothing reads the card until the first S2 session, and every battery until then reads a pick that loses the run.

## Decision
Build the measuring arm of item 4, then read three arms on one base and the same seeds, on the Tempest pair and the Arc Grid pair, each with the standard bot and with the arm on:
- the hold;
- the port's halo as it stands on `dev/s2`;
- the source's halo (D-071's branch, merged onto the base that holds the arm).

Then the scheduler rules on the form with those columns in hand:
- if the source's halo holds a run when the bot steps in (the two windows at or over 0.9 on the arm-on columns), option 2 has the reading item 7 asks for, and the halo can land as the source's with its cost on the card and the S2 kit's questions;
- if it does not, the additive form of item 1 is built, as the wall's was.

Why not build the additive form now: it would land a weapon that is the plain one with a ring this bot does not use, on the strength of a fault that is the instrument's as much as the weapon's. One small arm tells the two apart, and the same arm will serve the next contact or zone weapon.

Why not hold for people now: nothing reads the card until the first S2 session, and the standard batteries would keep reading a pick that loses the run.

## Hypothesis
We believe that a bot that holds its crowd at the carried weapon's reach lands the halo's blades on what it fights, because today the nearest enemy stands at a median 201 uu, the outer edge of blades that reach about 200, and the bot never closes the last step; measured by `Tools/BotRuns.ps1 -Carry Weapon.OrbitalDrones` with the arm on, 15 seeds, bot v2 at skill 1.0, on the source's halo: the weapon's hits per minute rise from 61 to at least 180, three times as many, and the nearest enemy's median falls inside the blades' reach; the arm is kept if both are met and taken out if either is missed, since an arm that does not make the bot use the weapon reads nothing. The halo's two windows, damage taken, deaths, reach and wins are printed on every column and decide nothing here: they are what the scheduler rules the form on.

- 180 is a floor for "the bot uses the weapon", not a target for the weapon: an enemy standing on the orbit is passed by a blade about three times a second (arithmetic), and the crowd holds a dozen or more.
- The arm judges itself and nothing else. No rule of play is judged on arm-on columns unless the scheduler's method decision says so.

## The perf bound
The arm changes what the bot weighs, not the sim: no bound is owed for it. The halo's own bound stays owed for whichever form lands, read with the perf scene's option the scheduler granted and put on hold (the Orbital Drones in the Spiral Projector's place when the option is on).

## Swap-out point
The first S2 session with people, for the halo's form. The arm itself stays as a measuring tool.

## Accepted with these rulings (scheduler, 2026-11-12)
1. Accepted as drafted. The arm lives on `dev/s2` and later branches only, off by default. `main`'s bot is not touched and session A is not reached.
2. With the arm off nothing moves, and the job shows it two ways: the seed 9107 hashes, and one standard battery equal run for run before and after.
3. The job first reads, read-only, whether the two terms it names are the whole of the bot's standoff, and stops and reports if the change is wider. The arm judges itself by its own hypothesis and is taken out if either measure is missed.
4. What a reading with the arm on may decide. A rule of play is still judged on the standard bot, because every other reading of this project is taken on it and a rule must not need a special bot to pass. A reading with the arm on is evidence of a second kind, and it answers one question only: does a weapon that gives up its rounds hold a run when the bot uses it, which is the reading D-074's item 7 asks for. For Blade Halo: if the source's halo reads at or over its bars with the arm on (0.9 before the boss; 0.9 in a fight that ten runs reach) on both pairs, the scheduler may land it as the source's, its cost stated on the card and asked in the S2 kit, by a record of its own; if it does not, the additive form is built. Neither follows by itself from this record.
5. The same arm is read on the wall when D-074 has its reading, printed only: a bot that stands in its disc is the nearest thing to a person who does.
6. Order and budget. It is S2 measuring equipment and not on the path to session A. It is built after the readings of D-074 and D-072 are in, since both built trees are in use, and it does not delay the M2 readings on `main`. One developer; if it meets trouble it waits.
7. If the arm needs a number of its own, it is a named constant of the bot with a number-log row, said before it is added, as drafted.
8. The switch and what it does are written in `bot.md` and printed in every battery's label that uses it, so that no reading with the arm on can be mistaken for a standard one.

## Result
- 2026-11-12, read by the Mechanics session on `s2/d075-bot-reach-arm` (kept, not handed over; `Validation/S2_d075_bot_reach_arm.md` on `dev/s2`). The arm missed its own clause on both builds and is out by ruling 3: on the source's halo, Tempest pair, the weapon's hits a minute read 69 and then 71 against a bar of 180 (the standard bot 61, the hold's plain drones 247).
- Ruling 3's read found the change wider than the draft's two terms: the term that sets the bot's standoff is the body term of the path's threat, which looks 0.6 s ahead. The first build left it whole; the second shortened it to the bot's own dash threshold. No third build was made.
- Printed: the nearest body's median 201, 187, 176 uu (standard bot, first build, second build; the blades reach a Swarm at 212); damage taken a minute before the boss 22.0, 34.1, 63.0 (the hold 1.7); died before the boss 7 of 15, 9 of 14, 8 of 11.
- With the arm off nothing moved on either build: the hold battery equal run for run, the five seed 9107 hashes `dev/s2`'s.
- What it leaves: no rule in `bot.md`, no test, no floor change. By ruling 4 the arm read nothing about the halo, since it never made the bot use the weapon; as printed evidence, a bot that stops walking away does not make a contact weapon work. The halo's form is D-079.
- One finding kept for later: R-BOT-09's `WeaponReach` reads the plain weapon's row for an evolved weapon, so the bot's card choice scores a Blade Halo by the drones' bolt range.
