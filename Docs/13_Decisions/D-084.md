# D-084 — Short texts take a display face of the game's own; all capitals only for a message that is important, short and neat

- **Date:** 2026-11-13
- **Decider:** the owner, in words and one picture (2026-11-13); the scheduler for scope, source and order (delegated, D-009)
- **Class:** RULE (`02_Systems/hud.md` R-HUD-08's type table and a rule of capitals to be written; `04_Presentation` R-PRE-10 for the font's source). It replaces the display half of the first visual wave's package G (`Validation/S4_wave1.md` on `dev/s4`)
- **Status:** accepted; designed and built by the Visual session on `dev/s4`

## Context
- Every text of the game is drawn on the engine's font. Fonts were the last package of the first visual wave (G: two families as OFL files from outside the repo) and were held: the Canvas path for a font that is not the engine's is unproved, and outside files on the public repo are the owner's ground (D-006).
- The owner's word of 2026-11-13, typed to the scheduler with a picture: "give this to Visual session, use this style of fonts in short texts, all upper cases should only be used in important, short, neat messages, other messages should not use all upper case".
- The picture is an alphabet of capitals in a very heavy, tightly condensed poster grotesque: tall narrow letters, round letters built as rounded rectangles, counters cut as narrow slots, a small spur at the top left of most stems. It reads as cut or stamped lettering. Its source and licence are unknown; it is kept outside the repository, as the art direction is.
- How many texts are all capitals today has not been counted.

## Options
- **A.** Fetch an OFL face near the picture's style (package G's road).
- **B.** Draw the game's own display face by a script in the repo, in the picture's spirit.
- **C.** Keep the engine's font and change only the capitals.

## Decision
B, with the owner's rule of capitals.

1. **A display face for short texts.** Titles, banners, names, headings, labels, buttons, and numbers where they read, are set in one heavy condensed display face. Reading text (descriptions, help lines, Codex bodies, Keeper lines) keeps a reading face, today the engine's. The Visual session draws the line between the two with a list of every text place in the game.
2. **The picture is direction and is not copied** (D-077). Its outlines are not traced and it never enters the repository. The Visual session designs the game's own face in its spirit and aims past it.
3. **The face is generated by a script in the repo** (R-PRE-10: procedural, licence own): capitals, lower case, digits and the punctuation the string tables use. The font file is written by a tool of the repo with the standard library only. No outside font file is fetched for it and nothing is installed. Every other asset of the look is made this way; a face of our own can be cut for this world and for the HUD's sizes; and an outside file stays the owner's step.
4. **If the own face cannot hold** (the engine does not take the written file, or the drawn face does not read at the HUD's sizes), the Visual session names one OFL face and the outside file goes to the owner as their step.
5. **The rule of capitals.** All capitals only for a message that is important, short and neat: an event or a result, of a few words, on one line. Every other text is not all capitals: labels, names, buttons, headings and descriptions are written in mixed case. The Visual session drafts the rule's sentence and its bound, with an audit of every string that is all capitals today and every place the code upper-cases a text, each marked keep or change, and a lint in `Tools` that fails an all-capitals string outside the allowed list.
6. **Chinese** has no case and no glyph in this face: zh-Hans short texts keep their present face, and the type sheet says how a zh short text carries the same weight. A CJK display face is not opened by this record.
7. **Where and when.** `dev/s4` only; the first stage's build on `main`, which session A is measured on, keeps its text. The first half needs no engine and starts at once: the type sheet (the alphabet; mock frames of the game's real places at 1920x1080 in en, one in zh-Hans), the audit, and the proof on a hidden desktop that Canvas draws and measures a font that is not the engine's. The build half (the type table, every width moves, every HUD and screen golden shot again) lands after the camera has reached `dev/s4`.
8. **Acceptance** is by eye on the game's frames at real size, the scheduler's and then the owner's (D-077); the lint and the layout tests are guards.

## Hypothesis
We believe that short texts in one heavy condensed face of the game's own, with capitals kept for the few messages that matter, make the screen read faster and look made rather than assembled, because weight then marks what is short and capitals mark what is important, instead of both marking everything; judged on the game's frames at real size by the scheduler and by the owner's eye; reverted to the engine's face if the drawn face does not read at the HUD's sizes.

## After the type sheet (scheduler, 2026-11-13)
The Visual session's design sheet came the same night: the alphabet in two cuts, a size ladder and four mock frames at 1920x1080. The scheduler opened every drawing. Accepted, with one change.
- **The face.** Letters cut from plates by one tool: every counter and aperture is a slot of the tool's width, round where it ends inside the plate. It takes the picture's weight, narrowness and slot counters and none of its signatures; its own are a waisted crossbar where two slots meet and a foot that leaves the plate at the baseline. A lower case designed first, one-storey a and g; two cuts from one construction, Display for titles and banners and Label for the HUD's sizes; digits of one width.
- **The one change: the Display cut is a step heavier.** The owner's picture is first of all mass: its stems measure about 0.19 to 0.24 of the capital's height and its counters about 0.08 to 0.11, where the sheet's Display cut has 0.17 and 0.12. Ruled: stems at about 0.20 to 0.21 with the slot at about 0.10, the letters as wide as that needs; if the slot closes at its smallest size the cut starts higher and the Label cut takes what is below. The two weights are shown side by side on a banner before outlines are drawn.
- **The rule of capitals, accepted as drafted:** "A text is set in all capitals only when it announces an event or a result (the boss's arrival, a win, a fall, a level gained), is at most three words and sixteen letters, and stands alone on one line. A label, a name, a card's title, a button and a description are never all capitals. Every other text is written in sentence case, and a name keeps its own capitals." Its rule id is given in `hud.md` with the build half.
- **The HUD's numbers** are set in the display face.
- **Chinese** as item 6 has it: the present face, a size about 1.15 times the Latin label beside it, wider spacing for a banner in place of capitals; a heavier weight only if the present face has one in the engine. Numbers, keys and Latin inside a Chinese line stay in the display face.
- Watched in the engine's proof, not settled by drawings: S against 5 and O against 0 at label sizes; labels over the lit floor (health and level are found at a glance, by a plate, a size or the reading face); the Label cut at 12 to 14 px on a moving fight.
- The owner got the alphabet and three mocks as what was made, with the change said.
- Not shown: any text drawn by the engine in this face.

## After the two weights and the audit (scheduler, 2026-11-13)
- **The Display cut is the heavier one:** stem 0.205 of the capital's height, slot 0.10, the O 0.51 of that height wide. The scheduler saw both weights side by side at real size and on the fight's frame: at 110 px the heavier word is a row of plates with slits where the first cut is a bold condensed word, and it still reads cleanly in lower case at 34 and 44 px. Its small counters begin to close at 28 px, so the Display cut starts at 32 px and the Label cut takes everything below; a banner is set at 32 px.
- **The audit** (`dev/s4`, read through git by a developer who changed nothing): 54 text places, all drawn on Canvas with one engine font object; proposed 40 display and 14 reading. The type table covers only the HUD: the offer, the hub and the menus each have their own painter and scales, and the boss's name and the elite labels draw outside every painter. Sixteen rows are all capitals today, and no banner, event or result is among them; the code never changes case. The en tables use 85 characters and lack J, 4 and 7. Chinese is drawn through the same font object.
- **Ruled on it:**
  1. The designations stay in capitals, as a named class on the allowed list, by key: the six Keepers' and ROOT, with the Codex bodies that quote them. They are one-word inscriptions from the stones, the same in both languages, and the capitals tell a mark from a name. The owner's rule is about messages; the owner is told that these were kept and why.
  2. Capitals are allowed, not required. The run's three great moments get them: short forms inside the bound are written for the boss's arrival, the win and the fall, en and zh (zh a first draft for the native reader), the present sentences staying as the line under a result. "Level up" goes to capitals. "Threat incoming" stays in sentence case, since it comes many times a run.
  3. The allowed list: HP, XP, the pad button names, the seven designation keys, `Level.Up` and the three event keys. The lint fails any other all-capitals string.
  4. The build half is two steps, each with its own whole gate, after the camera's picks are on `dev/s4`: one type table for the four painters and the two stray items on today's font, every golden unchanged and proved by them; then the faces switched in with the strings and the lint, every golden shot once.
  5. The face holds the whole alphabet, all ten digits and the nine marks outside ASCII, whatever the tables use today.
  6. If the engine does not fall back to the present face for Chinese inside one text item, the painter draws a string as runs. The proof's mixed string decides.

## After the engine proof (scheduler, 2026-11-13)
- **The own face can be made** (`s4/type-proof`, a placeholder face of boxes; it does not land): a font file written by the repository's own standard-library tool is taken by the engine, drawn by Canvas and measured to the pixel. The clock holds still on digits of one width; S and 5, O and 0, I, 1 and l, B and 8 stay apart at 14 to 21 px; over the lit floor a label in a runtime face of ours is found at a glance at 14, 16 and 18 px where the engine's font is slow at 16 and hard at 14 (the developer's eye and the Visual session's). Today's engine font is soft at title sizes; a runtime face is crisp at every size. So item 4, the fall-back to an outside font, is not needed.
- **The engine's own font importer crashes when run headless.** The bytes go in through the importer's own data path by an editor-only debug command: a workaround the project owns, written in `07_Technical` with the faces' hand-over, and absent from every game build.
- **Chinese is drawn by fallback inside one text item** (a font of ours with the present Chinese face as its fallback typeface); the painter does not split a string into runs. Chinese has one weight in the game's content, so size and spacing carry it; a second strike one pixel aside is tried on banners and headings in step two and judged on a frame.
- **Who draws the outlines.** The letters are the Visual session's, as recipes (every glyph a plate and its cuts, all 95 characters). A developer ports the recipes to exact outlines as `s4/type-faces`, two font files (Label, and Display at the heavier cut), with a test that the outlines are the recipes. A letter's shape changes only in the recipes and only by the Visual session's judgement on a frame; no outline is mended by hand. No kerning.
- **Owed at the first package of `dev/s4` after the faces land:** in a cooked build, that the hollow plate draws for a missing character, and that the Chinese fallback face is staged with the fonts; zh-Hans frames of the sheet.
- Not shown: the game's own letters drawn by the engine.
