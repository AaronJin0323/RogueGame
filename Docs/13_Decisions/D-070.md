- **Date:** 2026-11-12
- **Decider:** scheduler (delegated, D-009), on a draft by the Mechanics module session (D-055 item 4: draft 11, `Validation/pulse_wall_rounds_plan.md`), accepted on 2026-11-12 with the rulings at the end. The owner's word of 2026-11-11 applies: "ask for the reason, make your decision which helps the game the best"
- **Class:** RULE (`reforging.md` R-RFG-12, on `dev/s2`), with three cells of the row set to the source's values in the same step, each cited to the source's code and none tuned. It follows D-061 and D-067 and closes D-020's open point
- **Status:** not landed: its hypothesis was missed in the boss fight on both pairs (Result). Superseded on 2026-11-12 by D-074, which keeps its disc and gives the weapon its rounds back

## Context
- The card says "Rings hold as a wall, pushing enemies out." On bots a build that takes it deals 0.74 to 0.78 of what it deals without it. D-061 showed the wall's `dps` is not the cause; D-067 made the growing ring carry out what it crosses, missed both its measures and was reverted.
- Split at the boss's start, the run files that exist tell two stories (the plan note's tables; the carry pair, 15 seeds, bot v2 at skill 1.0). Before the boss the build stands at 0.80 to 0.92 of its hold and seven or eight runs of fifteen die there. In the boss fight it stands at 0.27 to 0.31: the evolved weapon deals the boss 170 to 717 a minute where the plain Ring Emitter dealt 5 793, Keeper.Storm loses half to three quarters of its damage with the hits it rode, and the fight lasts 124 to 136 s against 84.
- The source's code, read by the scheduler and by the drafter (`Source/RogueHero/Combat`, outside this repository; the note quotes the lines): the evolved weapon fires no rounds, as in the port; but its wall is a zone that strikes and knocks outward everything inside its whole radius every quarter second, for 1.5 of a round a tick. The port's wall strikes and pushes only its outer 100 uu and leaves its inside alone. Those two sentences are the port's own first guess (D-020 kept the value because the hand-over documents never state the wall's damage), and they are the two faults the files show: the crowd walks in, and the boss stands where nothing strikes.
- The port's cells beside the source's are one table in the note. In short: `band_uu` 100 against none; `dps` 2 against 6 in the port's unit; a steady push of 200 uu a second with no mass against a kick of 220 a tick that decays and is divided by mass, about 73 uu a second on a light enemy; the capabilities Projectile, Radial, Area and Expiry against Radial and Area.
- The drafter's earlier argument against a disc that hurts, that one `dps` cannot fit a crowd and a boss, is withdrawn in the note: a crowd in the disc is pushed out or dies, and before the boss the damage a build can deal is capped by the health that walks in, so the number has to fit the boss alone.

## Options
- **S — the wall as the source has it.** Each `tick_s`, every enemy inside the ring's current radius is struck and pushed radially outward; the boss is struck and not moved. Cells from the source: no band, `dps` 6. The capabilities become Radial and Area, so a modifier that needs a round goes dormant by the port's own rule, which shows it to the player and names it on the card before the pick.
- **C — the volley keeps its rounds** (Draft 10's recommendation). The rounds fire as the plain weapon's, and the port's band wall opens when none is live. It is `[invented]`: the source fires no rounds. No number moves.
- **B — a true wall for a hero who stands in it**: a band no walker crosses, with D-067's carry. It leaves the boss fight where it is.
- **D — hold the card for people**, or out of the offers (the scheduler decides that when the S2 kit is built).
- Draft 10's option A (the whole disc hurts, at the port's `dps`, without a push) is S with two of the source's cells missing; it is not kept as an option of its own.

## Decision
S first, then C if S misses its hypothesis or costs too much game thread. This is the scheduler's leaning, and I agree with it for three reasons: it is what the card says and what the source does, it goes at both faults with one rule, and the argument I had against it was about a number and was wrong.

- R-RFG-12 becomes: a volley is one ring at the hero, growing to `radius_uu` over `grow_s` and holding for `hold_s`. Each `tick_s`, every enemy inside the ring's current radius is struck for `dps` × the composed `Damage` × `tick_s`, one round's damage and not the weapon's whole output (D-020), as impacts so flags still react, and is pushed radially outward through displacements; an enemy that ignores displacement is struck and stays. One ring per weapon lives at a time; the cooldown is at least the ring's life.
- The free-volley clause of D-039 and D-040 keeps its sense: the live ring strikes once more what stands inside it, as that volley's source and at its scale.
- Cells: `band_uu` goes (the whole disc); `dps` 2 becomes 6, the source's 1.5 of a round a tick. `push_uu_s` stays 200 for the reading, applied once a tick as the source applies its kick, so one displacement per enemy per tick and not one per step: that is the source's rhythm and a fifteenth of the displacement load. On the whole disc 200 is stronger than the source's push (about 73 a second on a light enemy, arithmetic), and a Tank at 165 a second no longer reaches a hero who stands in the disc. Whether that is too safe is the first S2 session's question; if the scheduler wants the source's strength at once, the cell is 73 and I would take it.
- Capabilities: the row's become Radial and Area. Of the fourteen modifiers that fit the Ring Emitter (`matrix.csv`), by the catalog's words seven need a round (Pierce, Ricochet, Homing, Orbit Lock, Boomerang, Scatter Shell, Spread Wave): they go dormant and the HUD and the card say so. Three act on a kill and still act (Chain Trigger, Spillover, Refit). Four react to an impact (Split Core, Lingering Field, Backwash, Conduit), and under S there are far more impacts for them: what each does on a strike that is not a round is the job's first step, a table of the fourteen from code and the harness.
- The drawing: "two beaded arcs, the edges of the band, not a filled disc" was true of a ring whose inside is untouched. Under S the inside hurts. The job draws the one outer arc with the code it has; what a disc that strikes should look like is a question for the Visual session, named here and not decided.
- What is not taken from the source: the radius does not scale with an Area stat, rings do not overlap (one per weapon stays), and nothing is refunded.

## Hypothesis
We believe that making the wall the source's disc moves the build's damage per minute against its hold in two windows, each judged by itself: before the boss, from 0.80 to at least 0.95, because what walks into the disc is struck and pushed out where today it walks through a band and stands on the hero; and in the boss fight, from about 0.3 to at least 0.5, because the boss is struck four times a second for 1.5 of a round where today the wall deals it 170 a minute, and Keeper.Storm has hits to ride again; measured by `Tools/BotRuns.ps1 -Carry` on the Ring Emitter pair, 15 seeds, bot v2 at skill 1.0, read by the split of the run files at the boss's start that this note uses, which the job makes a part of `Tools/evolve_read.py` with a tools test so that the reading is a tool's and not a scratch script's; the one figure for the whole time after the pick, the nearest enemy and the damage taken are printed beside them and decide nothing; reach and wins printed and judged nowhere; reverted, the rule and its three cells put back, if the build reads under 0.95 of its hold before the boss or under 0.5 of it in the boss fight.

- Why 0.5 and not 0.9 in the fight: 0.9 is parity with a weapon that puts twenty-four rounds into a boss at arm's length, and a zone gives part of that up by what it is. 0.5 is what the source's own cells should give by my arithmetic. If the scheduler holds that the pick must not cost the fight at all, the bar is 0.9, S will probably miss it, and C should be built first.
- What follows a pass that is short of parity: one TUNE pass on `dps` upward, judged on the boss-fight window alone, as a step of its own by `10_Tuning.md §5`. The supply cap above says it cannot overshoot before the boss; the same reading shows whether that is so.
- What follows an overshoot: if the build lands above `REFORGE.POWER_MAX` (1.8) before the boss, one TUNE pass on `dps` downward, as its own step.
- What follows a miss: C, with Draft 10's hypothesis (at least 0.95 before the boss and at least 0.9 in the fight) and its test `Dusktracer.Reforging.PulseWallRounds`.

## Accepted with these rulings (scheduler, 2026-11-12)
1. Accepted: S first, the wall as the source has it. C, the volley that keeps its rounds, is the next draft to build if S misses its hypothesis or is stopped by the perf line. C is `[invented]`; the source fires no rounds.
2. The push takes the source's strength at once: `push_uu_s` is 73, flat for every role that can be moved, applied once a tick. Not 200. At 200 on the whole disc no walker reaches a hero who stands in it, which is the "too safe" wall we can already see by arithmetic, and we would be reading a number we expect to change. The source's strength is the one that was played. The port's displacement has no mass, so the value is flat; mass is its own rule if it is ever wanted. The number-log row shows the arithmetic from the source's lines (a kick of 220, its decay, the inverse mass). If the reading says the crowd still stands on the hero, a TUNE upward is a step of its own.
3. The two bars are accepted as the rule's own: at least 0.95 of the hold before the boss, at least 0.5 in the boss fight, each judged by itself. A pick that leaves the boss fight at half is not where this ends: a pass is followed by one TUNE pass on `dps` upward, judged on the fight window alone, whose aim is a fight at 0.9 of the hold or more. That step has its own hypothesis and its own revert.
4. Perf. The job's stop line stands as drafted: the S2 scene twice before and twice after, and it stops if the game thread at p95 rises by more than 0.3 ms or an after run fails the scene's budget. Beyond it: the scheduler reads the S2 scene alone on both builds before the branch lands, and the rule's cost is read again on `dev/s4` when it is carried there, where the S4 scene has no game thread left to give (D-056, D-068).

## Before the build: what the code says, and four more rulings (scheduler, 2026-11-12)
The job's first step, the table of the fourteen modifiers read from the code by the Mechanics session, corrects this record in three places.

- Under the capabilities Radial and Area eleven of the fourteen modifiers go dormant, not seven. Every flag of `flag_compat.csv` reads "none" on Radial and on Area, and a modifier whose flag does not resolve is refused. Chain Trigger, Spillover and Refit stay live. The Decision's sentence about "far more impacts" for Split Core, Lingering Field, Backwash and Conduit does not arise: they are dormant too. Nothing multiplies, so ruling 5 does not stop the job.
- What the port does today, for the record: a wall's strike places nothing, so Split Core, Scatter Shell and Lingering Field never fire on it; Backwash pulls each struck enemy toward the ring's centre, against the push; Conduit applies the Patron's status. Nine modifiers are live in name and do nothing. Shown dormancy is the better state.
- The Decision's sentence that `push_uu_s` "stays 200 for the reading" is replaced by ruling 2: the job takes 73.

10. Two cells of `flag_compat.csv` change with the rule: `Flag.StatusApply` and `Flag.Chain` read "native" in the Area column. They are the two Keeper reactions the code already runs on a ring's strike without asking whether their flag resolved; the cells make the catalog say what the code does. `Flag.StatusApply` is the port's name for what the source's wall keeps (its ApplyPatronStatus) and is cited to the source. `Flag.Chain` is the port's own: kept because it acts today and the hypothesis was reckoned with it; it is read again at the TUNE step. Conduit therefore stays live: ten dormant. The job shows the regenerated matrix's diff and stops if any cell of another weapon moves.
11. `weapons.md` R-WPN-08 and the test of `reforging.md` T-RFG-01 take a stated exception for this one row: its other flags resolve "none", and a "none" flag does nothing there. The exception names its end: the record on what happens to the modifiers an evolution refuses (ruling 7). When that rule lands, the exception is read again and goes if no socket can then hold a "none" flag.
12. A caveat on the second window, written before the reading so that it is no surprise. Keeper.Storm's fall in the boss fight is the loss of Tempest's Split children, and only a round's hit, a detonation or an expiry makes them. A ring's strike starts chains and no children, under the port's rule, under this one and in the source alike. So "Storm has hits to ride again" is true of chains only, and the fight may read under the drafter's 0.45 to 0.6. The bars do not move: at least 0.95 before the boss and at least 0.5 in the fight, and a miss reverts the rule and sends C to be built, as the hypothesis says.
13. The carry pair is a Tempest build, the hardest case for a weapon that fires no rounds. Beside it the job reads one more pair on the same weapon whose Keeper does not live on a round's children, with its own hold, the same seeds and the same split at the boss. It is printed and judged nowhere; it is there so that a miss can be told apart: a weapon that does not work, or a weapon that does not suit one archetype.
