## Options
- **A.** Keep paint and the two windows.
- **B with the pale line** (a): the contract held by brightness, the red given up.

## Decision
B, in the form of (b).

1. **Danger is drawn as light.** A warning is the striking machine's targeting light on the stone: an edge on the damage's own outline, whole from the first frame; a fill that grows from the source over the lead; chevrons where a thing comes along the ground. The fill adds to the stone and never covers it.
2. **The edge is a red line with a dark partner.** The line is saturated red and carries danger's hue; outside it lies a thin scorch, the stone's skin darkened and never painted black; the line's glow stays inside the line. Red is danger's alone, and it is seen as red.
3. **The fill carries the red mass:** 0.40 of the edge at its first value, growing as the clock. Under the squint test a warning holds at least three quarters of the red mass that paint holds on the same ground.
4. **The contract, for every point of a warning's outline on any ground from shadow to the contract's limit of lit stone:** one pair of the warning's own tones holds 3 to 1. First the red line against its scorch. If the scorch cannot be made to do it without reading as paint's black band, the line carries a pale hot centre inside the red, and that centre against the ground is the pair: a wire white-hot at its heart and red in its body. A warning must hold by luminance alone for an eye that sees little red; hue is the second sign and never the only one.
5. **The first tenth of a second is mass:** the whole shape at full strength, then the outline and the growing fill. Judged at the game's step spacing on the shortest lead, against paint.
6. **A thin shape is one solid line,** not its own outline: an aim line is a hot wire.
7. **The enemy's round** carries its own contrast as the second package's sheet has it: a core near black for bright ground and a rim bright enough for dark ground.
8. **The floor is freed.** The play floor is no longer bound to the two windows. Its bound is the screen contract: lit stone at most 0.20, shadow as deep as the design wants, and nothing of the floor so bright or so saturated that it could be taken for fire or a warning. The look's first package is built as the deeper, wetter bed.
9. **They land together.** A freed floor under painted warnings fails the contrast step, and it should. The first and second packages are gated on one merged tree, with `Tools/contrast_check.py` reading the warning as item 4 words it (granted to the second package's branch) and the squint figure of item 3 as a guard.
10. **What follows in the rules:** the sentences of R-PRE-03 and R-PRE-05 on the dark band and on how a two-tone element passes; `C.PRE.LIT_GROUND_Y_WINDOW` gives way to a ceiling; token rows for the line, the scorch's strength and the fill. `Pal.Telegraph` keeps its value for the hue. Written by the package's branch, checked at its hand-over.

## Hypothesis
We believe that a warning drawn as a red line with a dark partner and a red fill stops a player as surely as paint does while letting the floor be a place, because it keeps paint's two virtues (a mass of danger's red, and two tones of which one always holds) inside light; measured by the squint figure, by the contract's pairs on the brightest patch, the lit half and the dark half, and by the onset strip on the shortest lead; judged by the scheduler on the game's frames and, when people play it, by whether anyone is hit by a thing they say they did not see coming; reverted to paint, with the floor back inside the two windows, if the built warning cannot hold item 4 or people report it.
